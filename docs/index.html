How the assessment connects

Scope and assets. The design documents are converted into an inventory of systems, data, endpoints, interfaces, vendors, and processes.
Threats and attack paths. Each threat is tied to an architecture boundary, including a detailed stolen-laptop/no-MFA chain.
Risk and gaps. Risks use the shared 5×5 qualitative matrix, while gaps use NIST CSF 2.0 statuses: Not Addressed, Partially Addressed, or Fully Addressed.
Controls and decision. Findings become evidence requirements, policy statements, and a conditional go-live gate owned by the COO.

Framework and sources

NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 is used consistently as the single framework. The official NIST publication describes CSF 2.0 as a taxonomy of high-level cybersecurity outcomes for managing, assessing, prioritizing, and communicating cybersecurity risk.
